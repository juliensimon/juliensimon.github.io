Vent Mauvais

Author: Julien Simon

Date: September 24, 2026 · 46 min read

Source: https://www.airealist.ai/p/vent-mauvais

On June 16th, 2026, Arthur Mensch promised “a very exciting model to come this summer”, open-weight, with early access in July for key partners. Summer ended on September 23rd with no public release.[1] In its last days, on September 21st, Mistral’s own product account posted that GLM-5.3, a model trained by Z.ai in Beijing, was now in Vibe, the product Le Chat became, and “the default in the web app”.[2]

In “Chanson d’automne”, Verlaine wrote that turn of the season: the violins of autumn sob, and the poet goes off “au vent mauvais / Qui m’emporte / Deçà, delà, / Pareil à la / Feuille morte”, on the ill wind that carries him here and there like a dead leaf. A mistral is a wind, the cold north wind of the French Mediterranean.[3] But in this story, Mistral is the leaf, and it goes where the money blows: a frontier lab for its investors, a sovereign supplier for the states that buy it, and, since August, the host of Chinese models, one of them now the default.

Mistral has two kinds of customers. The states whose purchases you can read, Luxembourg and Hesse, bought it without a competitive tender and published the price to the euro. Companies buy it too, Airbus and HSBC among them; they announce the deal, and rarely the price, and no instrument outside their walls can see what they run.[4] Where a free choice can be measured at all, in a survey of German firms, on the Hugging Face download counter and on one developer marketplace, it goes to other models. And the valuation, more than €21 billion as of September 8th, 2026, rests on numbers nobody outside the company and its investors can read.[5]

Les Echos has spent September on a three-part “saga Mistral”, and on the 17th called the company “le champion qui pourrait abandonner la course à l’IA”, the champion that might drop out of the AI race.[6] The story has been told. What follows is the ledger: who pays for Mistral, how much, and who picks it when nobody makes them.

Who bought

On June 17th, 2025, Luxembourg’s state IT center, the CTIE, signed a contract with Mistral AI carrying a published value of €39,500,000. The notice on the EU’s tender platform records one tender received, no duration, and a single award criterion, weighted at 100 percent: “Absence de concurrents pour des raisons techniques”, no competitors for technical reasons. The justification lists five conditions the supplier had to meet simultaneously; taken together, they describe one company.[7]

Six weeks later, on July 28th, Luxembourg’s Direction de la Défense recorded a second award to Mistral, with a published value of €4,880,000, also without a call for competition, coded “technical”, and its only written justification is a single article of the Grand Duchy’s defense-procurement law.[8]

The third is German. On July 2nd, 2026, the Land of Hesse awarded €7,800,000 to Mistral after a documented market exploration and five mandatory requirements, including that the models the vendor trained itself were developed within the European Economic Area or a country with an EU adequacy decision. Hesse also published a voluntary transparency notice 45 days in advance, which is the step that allows a rival to challenge the award. None is on the record. The notice says the absence of competition is not the result of an artificial narrowing of the requirements but of the need as lawfully defined and the market as it stood.[9]

Fourteen months after its first purchase, the Grand Duchy joined Mistral’s cap table in the September 2026 Series D.[10] Nothing in the notices or the funding announcement connects the two, and three different arms of the state were involved.[11]

So that is the buying side: €52.18 million of published contract value, all of it from governments, none of it competed. On the French side, the state that treats Mistral as its national champion has published no award at all: I searched three registers for the company every year. What Les Echos reported on September 2nd, and what the ministry confirmed to Capital, is a €6 million, non-exclusive contract for 2026 and 2027, said to follow a public-procurement procedure; on September 22nd, it was in neither.[12]

What France has published is a defense framework with no amount,[13] and one costing from its own inspectors: about €300,000 a year for 10,000 active users on the state’s Mistral-powered assistant, and about €3 million a year if it went to one or two million, with Mistral named as the current supplier. French defense contracts can be withheld from publication. But the Luxembourg defense award was itself a negotiated defense contract, and Luxembourg published it, so the silence is a choice, or a contract carried under a reseller’s name; the registers cannot say which.

Mensch has put a split on the record, once, from memory. Asked under oath on May 12th what share of revenue public orders represent, he said 20 percent of software revenue, 10 percent for the French state, “de tête”, with compute carved out, and named Luxembourg unprompted as a significant framework customer.[14] No audited figure supports the 80 percent that leaves for private buyers.

Who chose

Bitkom, the German digital industry association, published a representative survey on September 9th, 2026, the day after Mistral announced its €3 billion round. Its research arm interviewed 603 German companies with 20 or more employees; 343 of them use AI, and those 343 form the basis of what follows. Asked which AI applications they use, 76 percent said ChatGPT, 35 percent Microsoft Copilot, 28 percent Gemini, 6 percent SAP’s Joule, 3 percent Claude. Mistral: none, and none the year before either, when the same question was put. The release says so in one sentence: no surveyed company reported using models from “des führenden europäischen Anbieters Mistral”, the leading European provider Mistral.[15]

This instrument measures brand names, not spend, which is why Claude scores 3 percent despite being a large enterprise API business. SAP’s Joule, at 6 percent, is not the clean comparator it looks like either: SAP has resold Mistral since 2024, and a German firm running Mistral through SAP answers “SAP”.[16] Le Chat, now Vibe, is a named end-user assistant, the kind of product a brand question sees best, and it read zero in both waves. And Mistral’s own “125+ global enterprises” is compatible with zero of 343 German firms: a hundred and twenty-five customers worldwide is a rounding error in a national sample.

The second instrument is Hugging Face, where the people who run models pull them. On September 22nd, 2026, I read the platform’s public counter. Mistral’s Apache-2.0 flagship, Mistral Large 3, a 675-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model released in December 2025, had been downloaded 2,097 times in the past 30 days and 13,457 times in its lifetime. Its four sibling repositories add 11,033 more; counted together, the gap to the comparator below is still 85 to one.[17] The fair comparison is another open model of the same size. DeepSeek-V3, 671 billion parameters, also a mixture of experts, released eleven months earlier in December 2024 and long past any launch spike, had been downloaded 1,118,995 times in the same thirty days, and 21 million times in its life.[18] Same size, both permitting commercial use, same window, same counter. A factor of about 530 on the flagship repository alone.

The counter counts file fetches, so a model pulled once into a company mirror counts once, and it does not see API usage at all. The counters tell you something narrower and, for a company with “open-weight” in the headline of its funding announcement, more awkward: the open flagship is the one almost nobody downloads. The smaller open-weight model, Medium 3.5, released in April under a modified MIT license, drew 89,194 pulls in the same window, forty times the flagship and a thirteenth of DeepSeek-V3.[17]

The third instrument is OpenRouter, the marketplace through which developers route requests to whichever model they choose; it does not see Mistral’s own API. Its chart of token share by the model lab’s country, posted by OpenRouter’s Peter Walker on September 21st, has France holding most of the platform’s tokens in early 2024, when Mistral’s small open models were the thing to run, 5 percent by the end of 2025, and no band wide enough to label since. On the same site’s live ranking of model authors by share of requests, Mistral stands eighth, at 2.6 percent, behind DeepSeek, Google, OpenAI, Z.ai, Qwen, Tencent and Anthropic. “Mistral really did have a moment there,” the post says. Developers with a choice are where the moment was.[19]

The first hour

The fourth instrument is my own hands. On August 17th, 2026, I installed Mistral Vibe, the company’s command-line coding agent, to build a small tool: pull the audio of a YouTube video with yt-dlp, then transcribe it with a Mistral speech model.

I asked Vibe for the transcription flow.[20] It was running Mistral Medium 3.5, the company’s newest general model, in Vibe since May 22nd, 2026. It told me that Mistral has no speech-to-text model, and steered me elsewhere. Voxtral has been public since July 15th, 2025, with two more speech models since then. The company’s own agent, on the company’s own three-month-old model, denied that it existed, and a developer who trusted the answer would have built on a competitor’s API. Training cutoff is not the excuse. The documentation is online, the company publishes anllms.txtindex of it for exactly this purpose, and the agent had a web-search tool in that session: it used it four times later in the same session, and not once before telling me the product did not exist. I re-ran the question on September 22nd on the same model, still the CLI’s default, in a single turn with web tools on, and the model gave the same answer: Mistral “does not currently offer a dedicated speech-to-text model”.

The fault is a broken window, not a model-quality problem, and the fix is cheap: an official Mistral documentation server that Vibe consults before it answers a question about Mistral.

What the founding memo said

Mistral’s founding strategic memo, seven pages, from before its first round in the spring of 2023, was published in full by Sifted on June 21st, 2023.[21]

The memo says where the founders thought the money was: “We believe that most of the value in the emerging generative AI market will be located in the hard-to-make technology, i.e., the generative models themselves.” It says what they promised: to become “a research leader in the generative AI field, eventually offering the best technology within 4 years”, which puts the deadline in April 2027.

And it says how they planned to compete: “Training a competitive model requires at least an exa-scale cluster for a few months. We intend to rent such capacity for a full year.” So this was never a plan to win on scale, and the founders said so on page five. It was a plan to win on efficiency: “know-hows that will allow us to gain a factor 10-100 in training efficiency compared to public methods”, so that the team could “train the strongest model for a given computational budget”.[22]

The open-source promise was hedged from the start: “We will balance our open-source strategy with our economic interests, keeping the strongest and most specialized models reserved for negotiated access.” And the route to market was a network, stated plainly: the early investors “will open all necessary doors for acquiring high-quality datasets”, and “the founding team is already organizing business exploration with major French and European industrial actors”.[23] Doors and introductions, in April 2023. Not a drift.

On June 16th, 2026, Arthur Mensch posted on LinkedIn that Mistral had “spent a few percent of the deployment of some of our competitors, in a domain that is fundamentally governed by flops budget”, and that “today, we do not yet own the best language models”.[1] The founding bet was that efficiency would beat a budget; in 2026 the founder wrote that budget governs. I won’t call that a contradiction; a founder is allowed to learn in three years.

What he told whom

On January 14th, 2026, on an American podcast, Mensch agreed with his host that frontier models were commoditizing and gave the reason: “the knowledge unit to actually train a model is fairly short, so because it’s short, it actually circulates.” The word was his host’s; the reasoning was his.[24]

On May 12th, 2026, under oath before a French parliamentary commission of inquiry, he described the value chain the other way up. The high-margin layer, the one that pays for research, “c’est l’intelligence artificielle”, is AI; the rest of cloud services “sont surtout des commodités”, are mostly commodities. In 10,547 words of testimony, the word “commodité”, commodity, appears once, and it points at the cloud, not at the models.[14] His own bridge between the two is on the same podcast, at 23:01: “to get to value, they need to have great models. So the two things are extremely linked together.”[24]

On September 8th, 2026, the funding announcement that raised €3 billion described Mistral as “the only AI company in the world building the full stack”. It is not in any of the nine archived snapshots of the company’s home page from May 2023 to September 2026.[25]

The September 21st post does not say which tiers get the Chinese model; the free tier’s command-line client did not offer it when I looked the next day.[2] Mistral’s newsroom, pricing page, and product page say nothing about it; the model’s own documentation page, dated September 15th, says it is “served without Mistral modifications” and prices it at Z.ai’s own list for input and output.[26]

On September 18th, three days before that post, Juan Branco, a left-wing lawyer and agitator, posted that Mistral lived on public orders pushed by the Élysée and served its clients “des modèles chinois, librement téléchargeables, d’avant dernière génération”, freely downloadable Chinese models of the previous generation. Mistral had announced the Chinese models itself, on August 11th. Its own documentation had made “previous generation” stale on September 15th, and Branco’s rebuttal narrowed it to GLM 5.2 “pendant tout un été”, all summer long, which was true of the model if not of the whole summer.[27] Mensch answered twice on September 20th: “Nos travaux déplaisent à certaines petites frappes”, our work displeases certain small-time thugs, and “c’est un obsessionnel”, the man is obsessive. He contested no figure and no fact, and by September 22nd had deleted both posts. A buyer at Airbus or HSBC reads the same feed, and sees the founder of a €21 billion company, accused in public of living on state orders, answer with an insult rather than the 20 percent he had given the Assemblée under oath in May.

The rolodex, counted

My May piece argued that Mistral was converting a perishable contact list into permanent law. Since then, I have counted the list, and the honest result is smaller than the phrase suggests.

Mistral names 23 investors in its Series D, and CMA CGM’s own release adds a 24th, from 2023.[28] Its customer pages name 42 companies, and partner, compute, and September’s announcements add eleven, for 53 commercial names against a claimed “125+”.[29] Seven names are on both lists: ASML, CMA CGM, Luxembourg, BNP Paribas, Belfius, Nvidia, and Samsung, which led the Series D and announced the next day that it would deploy Mistral Large on premises across its chip operations.[5] Five of the seven are on the customer pages; the other 37 customers there, Airbus, HSBC, Capgemini, and on down the list, have no equity link in any document I read.[30]

So the cap table does not explain the customer list. It explains four of the seven, the ones where a stake is large, or a purchase has a price: Samsung, which led the round, and these three. ASML led the Series C with €1.3 billion for about 11 percent; it is a named customer and a compute anchor; its own audited filing carries the stake at €1,320.7 million, and its announcement prices the commercial side at nothing: an agreement to “explore the use of AI models”.[31] CMA CGM invested in 2023, anchors the compute plan, and published a figure Mistral never has: a five-year partnership “supported by a €100 million investment”, which the group’s releases never split between its own program and what it pays Mistral.[32] And Luxembourg bought twice, without competition, then invested.

Of 56 relationships announced by Mistral or its counterparties, ten have a published value, with eleven figures in total because Microsoft appears twice, and three of the eleven are words rather than numbers. Three are the procurement notices, exact to the euro. Two are equity: ASML’s audited stake and Microsoft’s 2024 convertible bonds, a figure neither party announced, reported by TechCrunch the next day and later printed by two competition authorities. Two are acquisition prices: Mistral’s own “several million euros” for Pimento, and a press report on Emmi AI that neither party confirmed. And four are private commercial deals whose size a party chose to publish: CMA CGM’s €100 million, by the buyer; TotalEnergies’ “more than €100 million” over three years, by the buyer, on September 15th; HUMAIN’s, “in the hundreds of millions of Euros”; and Microsoft’s compute agreement of July 2026, “a multibillion dollar commitment” in Microsoft’s own release, with no figure, no term, and no mention in the 10-K Microsoft filed eight days later.[33] So the only exact, audited euro figures associated with Mistral are the amounts others paid for its shares. What anyone pays for its product is a procurement notice, a buyer’s press release, or an adjective. And no revenue figure is filed or audited anywhere: Mistral AI SAS has never deposited annual accounts in France, and no counterparty’s filing states a contract value.[4]

What actually exists

The commoditization diagnosis was right, and Mensch made it in January, before his own numbers made it visible. His company’s speech model, Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime, is a genuine world-class franchise: 1,746,931 downloads in thirty days and 11.4 million since January 2026, the only post-2025 model in the company’s top three.[34] Its 2023 and 2024 small models had a publisher’s distribution, with 64 million and 46 million lifetime pulls for the two 7B versions.[35] Mistral has had users who signed nothing, in the millions. They came for the small models and for speech, and the 2025 flagship did not inherit them. And there is a documented repeat buyer: BNP Paribas, also an investor, renewed for three years in May 2026.[4]

The flagship line reopened under Apache 2.0 with Mistral Large 3, 21 months after the closure of Mistral Large in February 2024.[36] And the domain he named in his June post, “voice, vision and document processing”, is where the company’s measured strength actually is. He knows which of his products work. His funding announcement sells the other one.

The label, written down

The institutions that designated the champion wrote the label down before they measured the product. Thirty days after Mistral shipped its flagship closed, API-only, and first on Microsoft Azure, in February 2024, the European Parliament’s research service listed Mistral AI as “Open source”, on the strength of Mistral 7B, the only model it named.[37][38] France’s competition authority did look: its opinion of June 28th, 2024 recorded that Mistral had not released Mistral Large as open weights, set out the test for control, and did not apply it to Microsoft’s deal.[39] And on September 2nd, 2026, the economy minister told AFP: “Si l’IA européenne se résume à Mistral, alors nous sommes fichus”, if European AI comes down to Mistral, we are done for.[40]

What would have to break

In March I wrote that Mistral existed despite the state.[41] In May I relayed the company’s reported $400 million of annual recurring revenue, with a billion targeted for the year.[42] Four months on, nobody outside the company and its investors can confirm either figure. Aleph Alpha, the company that Germany’s press and ministers treated as its champion, filed its accounts for 2022, 2023 and 2024 before signing this month to merge with Cohere. [43] Mistral AI SAS has filed none, ever.[4] The French state holds shares through Bpifrance,[5] so it can read the accounts Mistral has not filed; the public cannot.

The argument fails if any of three things happens:

  1. If Mistral files audited accounts in France, the valuation no longer rests on numbers nobody outside can read; and if they put private customers near the 80 percent of software revenue that Mensch’s sworn 20 percent leaves them.

  2. If any Apache-2.0 Mistral flagship passes 100,000 thirty-day downloads on the Hugging Face counter, or the DeepSeek-V3 gap falls below ten to one, the choosing side is wrong.

  3. If a general-purpose Mistral model ships before the memo’s April 2027 deadline and scores above, say, 30 on Artificial Analysis’s intelligence index, against 14 for its best today and 45 for the Chinese model it now serves, the efficiency bet is back on.

Until then, the picture is the one the documents draw: states buying without a tender, seven investors doubling as customers or partners, a free choice going elsewhere wherever it can be measured, and private contracts nobody outside can price. The four-year clock the founders set themselves runs out in seven months, with no general-purpose model released since April 28th, and, by its own product account, a Chinese one as the web app’s default. Deçà, delà.
